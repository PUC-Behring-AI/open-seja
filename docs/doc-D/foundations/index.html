Vol. 5: Foundations and Philosophy Phase 2

This volume is in draft status. All seven chapters contain authored content with publication-quality standards.

This volume presents the intellectual foundations that SEJA is built upon. It traces the lineage from de Souza's semiotic engineering through sign systems and metacommunication theory to the practical principles -- abductive reasoning, reflective practice, and epistemic governance -- that shape how SEJA structures human-agent collaboration. This is the conceptual depth layer for researchers, contributors, and practitioners who want to understand why SEJA works the way it does.

Chapters

  1. 1. Software as Designed Communication De Souza's central thesis that software is a one-shot message from designer to user, delivered by the interface acting as the designer's deputy. Shows how SEJA's harness files serve as the designer's deputy for the AI agent, with a critical counter-reading on the single-author assumption and Eco's "open work."
  2. 2. Sign Systems and Communicability The three sign classes from semiotic engineering -- static, dynamic, and metalinguistic -- mapped to concrete SEJA artifacts. Examines how SKILL.md frontmatter, lifecycle transitions, and the /explain skill each function as different classes of signs, with a counter-reading on classification boundary problems and Eco's signification-vs-communication distinction.
  3. 3. The Extended Metacommunication Template Barbosa et al.'s (2021) extension of the metacommunication template to four lifecycle dimensions -- conception, design, implementation, and evaluation -- with ethical questions at each stage. Maps SEJA's constitution, /design interview, and decision records to the extended template, with a counter-reading on the gap between prescribing and enforcing ethical reflection.
  4. 4. Reflective Practice Schon's three registers of reflection -- knowing-in-action, reflection-in-action, and reflection-on-action -- operationalized through SEJA's reference files, research workflow, and /reflect skill. Covers all three /reflect modes (conversational, deep, telemetry) and the non-prescriptive rule, with counter-readings from Eraut and Boud on the limits of structured reflection.
  5. 5. Abduction and Plural Readings Peirce's abductive reasoning as the interpretive stance behind how agents read designer intent, operationalized through SEJA's council-debate mechanism with five expert archetypes. Examines provisionality in the promote workflow and the deliberate surfacing of tensions over premature consensus, with a counter-reading on simulated vs. genuine communities of inquiry.
  6. 6. The Governable and Epistemic Thesis The dual thesis from constitution Q3 that SEJA must be both governable and epistemic -- governance without epistemic support produces brittleness; epistemic support without governance produces drift. Inventories concrete harness features on each axis and examines their mutual constitution, with a counter-reading on the maturity asymmetry and independence assumption.
  7. 7. How the Agent Reads the Harness Meta-documentation on how Claude processes harness files through the finite context window. Covers pre-skill's ref-load mechanics, the eager/lazy loading two-tier strategy, pinned anchors that survive compaction, and the implications for harness authors -- why conciseness, structure, and file naming conventions are epistemically significant, not merely stylistic.

Recommended Reading Order

Start with "Software as Designed Communication" for the foundational premise, then "Sign Systems and Communicability" and "The Extended Metacommunication Template" for the theoretical framework. "Reflective Practice" and "Abduction and Plural Readings" cover the reasoning models that inform SEJA's lifecycle. "The Governable and Epistemic Thesis" synthesizes the theoretical commitments, and "How the Agent Reads the Harness" grounds the theory in SEJA's concrete runtime behavior. Readers coming from Vol. 1 may prefer to start with "How the Agent Reads the Harness" and work backward.

Related Volumes

The theory presented here directly informs Vol. 2: The Design Layer, where these concepts are instantiated as project artifacts. Newcomers who arrived here from the collection index may want to start with Vol. 1: Onboarding and Orientation first for practical context before engaging with the theory.
