How to Reflect

Goal: step back and look at specific work you have done, through one of two explicit lenses -- the product lens (what you built: does it do what you intended?) or the practice lens (how you worked: what patterns emerge in how you designed and built it?). The /reflect skill records your reflection in your own words. It has three modes: conversational (default), deep (--deep, visualization-backed practice narrative), and telemetry (--telemetry, statistical pattern mining).

When to Use

  • You want to examine what you built and ask whether it does what you intended (product lens).
  • You want to examine how you worked -- where you hesitated, what you would do differently (practice lens).
  • You want a visual timeline and narrative of practice evolution over a period (use --deep).
  • You want statistical pattern mining across weeks of usage data (use --telemetry).

Required Inputs

  • A scope: recent plans, recent research, a specific artifact ID, a time window, or free-form.
  • For deep and telemetry modes, an optional time window (--since 14d) and scope keyword or --skill <name> filter.

Step by Step

  1. Pick a mode.
    • Conversational (default) -- Recommended when you want to write a reflection in your own words about specific artifacts. NOT recommended when you want statistics rather than narrative.
    • Deep (--deep) -- Recommended when you want a visual, narrative chronicle of how your practice evolved across a period. NOT recommended for a quick reflection on one artifact.
    • Telemetry (--telemetry) -- Recommended when you want statistical pattern mining (sequence frequency, duration outliers, revision density, stuck-loop detection, decision reversals). NOT recommended when you want a written personal reflection.
  2. Choose a scope and lens (conversational mode). Pick the artifacts; the skill summarizes them, then asks whether you are reflecting through the product lens, the practice lens, or both, and poses the lens-appropriate open-ended question.
    Expected output: your reflection saved to _output/reflections/reflection-<id>-<slug>.md, recording which lens was used.
  3. Run deep or telemetry mode for aggregate views. Deep mode produces an event-matrix visualization, a transition graph, and a past-tense practice-evolution narrative. Telemetry mode runs five analysis primitives over telemetry.jsonl.
    Expected output: a markdown report under _output/reflections/, with companion visualization files in deep mode.

Tip. The practice lens is Schön's reflection-on-practice: the point is not to grade the artifact but to notice the patterns in how you worked so you can adjust as a practitioner.

Expected Outputs

  • A reflection-<id>-<slug>.md report in _output/reflections/, with the lens recorded.
  • In deep mode, companion visualization files (event matrix and transition graph).
  • In telemetry mode, a statistical report (printed to stdout with --dry-run).

Common Mistakes

MistakeBetter approach
Letting the agent write the reflection for you. /reflect records your words verbatim. The value is in your own account, not a generated summary.
Blurring the two lenses. Choose deliberately: product lens for what you built, practice lens for how you worked. The report records which one.
Using --telemetry when you wanted a personal reflection. Telemetry is statistical pattern mining. For a written reflection, use conversational mode.
Reflecting but never acting on what surfaced. Follow up with /research, /design, or /plan to turn a pattern into new intent or work.
