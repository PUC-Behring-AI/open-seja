How to Research a Question

Goal: get a grounded answer to a project question -- a design trade-off, an architectural choice, or "what exists in the codebase?" -- before you commit to changing anything. The /research skill investigates your codebase and design files, analyzes the question from multiple perspectives, and returns prioritized recommendations. From iteration 2 onward it is the usual entry point to the lifecycle.

When to Use

  • You need guidance on a design decision and want the trade-offs surfaced before you plan.
  • You are unsure of the best approach and want a recommendation grounded in the actual codebase.
  • You need an overview of what already exists (use --inventory to catalog elements matching a pattern).
  • The decision is high-stakes and benefits from a structured multi-perspective debate (use --deep).

Not for generating artifacts someone else will read (use /document, /communicate, or /onboard) and not for turning a decision into a build plan (use /plan). /research answers your questions.

Required Inputs

  • A question or topic, e.g. /research Should the settings flow be a modal or a full page?
  • For inventory mode, a pattern to catalog, e.g. /research --inventory List all API endpoints and their HTTP methods.
  • A project with design intent files already populated helps, but is not required -- research reads whatever context exists.

Step by Step

  1. Pick a mode. Default (research) is question-driven; --inventory produces a codebase catalog; --deep convenes a structured expert council debate.
    Expected output: the skill confirms the mode it will run.
  2. Run the skill with your question. The agent researches the codebase and evaluates the question from relevant perspectives (for example UX, accessibility, architecture), then writes a structured report under _output/research-logs/ as research-<id>-*.md.
    Expected output: a report with perspective analysis and recommendations tagged HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW priority, plus 2-5 auto-derived topic tags.
  3. Handle any detected design decision. If the Q&A surfaces a design decision, the skill offers a decision-extract flow.
    • Create a D-NNN entry now -- Recommended when the decision is settled and you want it recorded in product-design/product-design-as-intended.md immediately. NOT recommended when the decision is still tentative.
    • Defer to the pending ledger -- Recommended when you want to record it but review the wording later.
    • Skip -- Recommended when the exchange was exploratory and captured nothing decision-worthy.
  4. Ask follow-up questions. Continue the Q&A within the same session to drill deeper; the exchanges are logged to the same report.

Expected Outputs

  • A research-<id>-*.md report in _output/research-logs/ with perspective analysis and prioritized recommendations.
  • Optionally a new ### D-NNN: Decision entry appended to product-design/product-design-as-intended.md (via apply_marker.py) if you accepted the decision-extract proposal.
  • In inventory mode, a structured catalog of all elements matching your pattern.

Common Mistakes

MistakeBetter approach
Using /research to produce documentation for others. Research answers your own questions. Use /document, /communicate, or /onboard for reader-facing artifacts.
Reaching for --deep on every question. Reserve the council debate for high-stakes, genuinely contested decisions; routine questions do not need 5-7 archetypes.
Skipping the decision-extract proposal, then losing the rationale. If a decision was actually made, capture it now or defer it to the pending ledger so it is not lost.
Treating a recommendation as a build plan. Recommendations are inputs to /plan. Move to /plan to turn them into executable steps.
