UX Research Artifacts

The metacommunication message says who I understand you to be and what I have learned you want or need to do. Those are empirical claims -- and empirical claims need evidence. SEJA keeps that evidence in a single Human (markers) file, product-design/ux-research-results.md: personas (R-P-NNN), problem scenarios (R-PS-NNN), a cross-reference map, a processing-status table, and discovered user journeys (JM-E-NNN). This chapter is a reference to the artifacts as they actually exist in SEJA's own research file, a how-to for populating them through /design, and an explanation of how this evidence base grounds the designer's first-person "I want you to…" statement in observation rather than assumption.

The Research File

All UX research artifacts live in one file: product-design/ux-research-results.md. It is classified Human (markers) -- prose (personas, problem scenarios, journey observations) is authored only by the designer or researcher; agents may write fixed-format lifecycle markers (INCORPORATED, CHANGELOG_APPEND) and only through apply_marker.py after an AskUserQuestion confirmation. Direct agent prose writes are rejected by check_human_markers_only.py during post-skill.

The file has three stable ID namespaces, and the IDs never move: an entry revised in place keeps its ID; an entry that is superseded mints a new one.

Prefix Artifact Section
R-P-NNN Persona §1 Personas
R-PS-NNN Problem scenario §2 Problem Scenarios
JM-E-NNN Discovered (empirical) user journey §5 Discovered User Journeys

The file's own section layout is: §1 Personas, §2 Problem Scenarios, §3 Cross-Reference Map, §4 Processing Status, §5 Discovered User Journeys, and a trailing append-only ## CHANGELOG. The subsections that follow walk each of these using the artifacts that are actually recorded in SEJA's research file.

Note: The JM-E-NNN journeys in §5 are the empirical, field-observed journeys -- what users actually do. They are distinct from the designed journeys (JM-TB-NNN) in product-design-as-intended.md §15 (what the designer intends) and the implemented journeys in product-design-as-coded.md § Journey Maps (what was built). The flow is one-directional: discovered journeys inform designed journeys, never the reverse.

Personas (§1)

A persona is a user archetype with goals, frustrations, and context. SEJA's own research file records three personas, all confirmed during the /design Mode 5 interview (2026-06-28). Each persona carries a set of goals with stable G-NNN IDs -- these are the machine-readable anchor for the metacommunication message's "what I've learned you want or need to do" clause (see Grounding the Message).

ID Name Role / Archetype Primary goal
R-P-001 Ana Semiotic Engineer-Designer Bridge design theory and AI-assisted implementation without losing the metacommunication thread
R-P-002 Beto Pragmatic Developer Build well-structured software with AI governance that requires no per-session context restating
R-P-003 Clara Tech Lead / Architect Maintain design coherence and surface drift across a team or codebase over time

Each persona entry expands the inventory row with a bio, a goal list, key frustrations, and relevant context (technical proficiency, usage frequency, domain knowledge). The three personas span the axis SEJA is built for -- from the theorist to the pragmatist to the governor:

R-P-001 Ana — the theorist
A semiotic-engineering graduate who treats the metacommunication message as the primary design artifact. Her goals: encode design intent as a machine-readable artifact (G-001), detect drift from the message before it is expensive to reverse (G-002), and keep an honest picture of what she does and does not yet know about her users (G-003). Her frustration is with AI tools that treat design documents as optional context rather than authoritative constraints.
R-P-002 Beto — the pragmatist
A senior engineer who wants governance without ceremony. His goals: AI help that stays within his architectural choices without re-explaining them each session (G-004), structured feedback on whether the implementation matches the plan (G-005), and transparency about why the AI chose an approach (G-006). His frustration is pasting design context into every new conversation because state does not persist.
R-P-003 Clara — the governor
A tech lead responsible for design coherence over time. Her goals: surface drift early (G-007), keep a shared authoritative decision record that survives personnel changes (G-008), and generate stakeholder-appropriate material from design intent without rewriting it per audience (G-009). Her frustration is design decisions that live in someone's head or a chat thread but not in the codebase.

Problem Scenarios (§2)

A problem scenario is a narrative of user pain without SEJA (or with an earlier version of it). Each is tied to one or more personas and their G-NNN goals, with a setting and a concrete story. SEJA's research file records three, pre-populated from design-interview knowledge and to be enriched with field observation as the harness is used in production.

ID Title Personas Goals The pain
R-PS-001 Design Intent Lost in Translation R-P-001, R-P-002 G-001, G-002, G-004 A new AI conversation has no memory of last session's design decisions; the AI produces a working implementation that makes a structural choice the designer explicitly rejected, and the drift compounds across the team.
R-PS-002 The Gap That Wasn't Surfaced R-P-001, R-P-003 G-003, G-007 A critique reports "all designed features are implemented" -- technically true -- but two problem scenarios from the research layer were never addressed in the design intent, leaving a missing research-to-design linkage that nothing traverses.
R-PS-003 Stakeholder Communication from Scratch R-P-003 G-009 Detailed design-intent documents, plan histories, and decision records exist, but none are in a stakeholder-ready format; the designer rebuilds slides by hand each quarter, and they go stale the moment the design evolves.

These scenarios are not decorative. R-PS-002 is the scenario that directly motivates SEJA's product-topology gap-detection feature, and R-PS-003 is what motivates the /communicate skill -- the research artifacts are the documented origin of design decisions, not an afterthought to them.

Cross-Reference Map (§3) and Processing Status (§4)

Two tables connect the research artifacts to the rest of the design layer and track their incorporation.

Cross-Reference Map

The map (§3) records, for each artifact ID, which design artifact it feeds and the relationship. In SEJA's own file, all three personas feed the Extended Metacommunication Template question 1.1 ("what I know about you") in product-design-as-intended.md §12 EMT 1.1; the problem scenarios feed EMT 1.3 (contexts of use) and, for R-PS-002 and R-PS-003, the specific features they motivate (product topology and /communicate). The relationship is Feeds: research is upstream, design intent is downstream.

Processing Status

The status table (§4) is a convenience view over the inline INCORPORATED markers on each entry -- the authoritative record is the marker on the heading itself, applied by post-skill via apply_marker.py after confirmation. In SEJA's own file, all six entries are currently pending: created via the /design Mode 5 interview but not yet incorporated into a plan. When a persona or scenario is incorporated into a design iteration, post-skill proposes an <!-- INCORPORATED: plan-NNNNNN | YYYY-MM-DD --> marker via AskUserQuestion, and the status flips.

Discovered User Journeys (§5)

Discovered journeys (JM-E-NNN) are journey maps observed through field study of the system in actual use -- contextual inquiry, usability studies, diary studies. Each entry carries a required research source (method, date, sample size), optional persona and problem-scenario links, a goal, pre-conditions, and a step-by-step observation table (observation, touchpoint, user emotion, pain point, source). They are empirical: they describe what users do, not what was designed or built.

In SEJA's own research file, §5 is currently empty by design:

No discovered journeys recorded yet. Populate from field observation as SEJA matures.

This is honest, not a placeholder to be filled with invented data. The section is structurally append-only for prose: check_changelog_append_only.py (run by the fast preflight gate at post-skill step 6b) diffs it against the prior commit with a prose-only rule, so apply_marker.py can insert INCORPORATED markers above existing JM-E-NNN headings without tripping a middle-insertion violation -- but no prose line can be removed, modified, or inserted in the middle. Only prose additions at the end are allowed. The emptiness itself carries meaning: SEJA has designed journeys (JM-TB-NNN) but has not yet observed field journeys, and the file says so plainly rather than pretending otherwise.

Populating the Artifacts via /design

The research file is instantiated and populated through /design. You never write the artifacts by hand into an agent context -- the Human (markers) classification forbids agent prose writes, and the designer authors the prose. Here is how the artifacts come to exist.

Where the file comes from

During template instantiation, /design copies template/ux-research-results.md to product-design/ux-research-results.md. It pre-populates personas and user-community details from the questionnaire's persona/user question (Q2.10) and the target-user question (Q2.1) when those were provided. The instantiated file keeps its Human (markers) classification, so from that point on the designer owns the prose and agents contribute only markers.

The interview path (Mode 5)

SEJA's own personas and scenarios were captured through Mode 5 (Interview-Driven), triggered by /design --mode interview. Mode 5 is a conversational intake rather than a fixed form:

  • Phase A -- Open-ended interview: the skill asks adaptive questions covering platform purpose, target users, domain entities, permissions, constraints, and edge cases, recording each exchange as a verbatim triple (agent question, user response verbatim, agent interpretation) in a transcript file.
  • Phase B -- Synthesis: the skill enumerates every field in its Field Classification and assigns a confidence label to each: DS (directly stated), INF (inferred), or PAC (proposed-and-confirmed).
  • Phase C -- Confirmation: security-critical inferred fields must be confirmed to DS or PAC via AskUserQuestion; other inferred fields are reviewed in a batch; gaps are filled with a single focused question each.
  • Phase D -- Instantiation: the confirmed values feed template instantiation (the same step described above), writing the personas and problem scenarios into the research file.

The confidence labels are why SEJA's personas can honestly say "all three were confirmed during the /design Mode 5 interview" -- the label records that these were not silently assumed but stated or confirmed by the designer.

Populating discovered journeys later

Personas and problem scenarios can be seeded at design time from interview knowledge, but discovered journeys (JM-E-NNN) come from field observation and are added as the system is used. You append them to §5 following the entry pattern (research source, persona/scenario links, goal, pre-conditions, an observation-step table, and key findings). Because §5 is append-only, you add new entries at the end; you do not rewrite what is already there.

Marking incorporation

When a persona, scenario, or journey is used in a plan, you do not hand-edit the status. Post-skill detects the incorporation and proposes an INCORPORATED marker via AskUserQuestion; on confirmation, apply_marker.py writes it above the entry's heading and appends a ## CHANGELOG line. That is the only write path for agents into this file.

Tip: To keep research honest about what you do not yet know, record gaps in the design intent's Extended Metacommunication Template rather than fabricating a persona detail. EMT question 1.1 explicitly asks "what do I not know about you?" -- an empty §5 or a sparse persona is a legitimate, recorded state, not a defect to be papered over.

Grounding the Metacommunication Message

The metacommunication message (Chapter 1) is the highest-authority artifact in the design layer. It is voiced in first person: "Here is my understanding of who you are… I've learned that what you want and need is…" Those two clauses -- who you are and what I've learned you want or need -- are claims about real users. The research artifacts are what keep them honest.

From assumption to evidence

Without a research base, "who you are" is an assumption the designer holds privately, and "what you want" is a guess. SEJA's message does not float free of evidence: its "who you are" clause is anchored in the three personas (R-P-001 Ana, R-P-002 Beto, R-P-003 Clara), and its "what you want or need to do" clause is anchored in their goals (G-001 through G-009). The Extended Metacommunication Template makes the linkage explicit -- its question 1.1 ("what do I know or don't know about you") points directly at ux-research-results.md §1–§4, and its design section maps each goal to the feature designed to serve it.

The one-directional evidence flow

Evidence flows upward into the message and never the reverse. The research file's Cross-Reference Map records each artifact's relationship to design intent as Feeds: personas feed the "who you are" understanding, problem scenarios feed the contexts of use and the features that answer them, and discovered journeys feed the designed journeys. If the designer changes the message, that is a design act; it does not retroactively rewrite the observations that grounded it. This is why the research file is authored by the human and only marker-annotated by agents -- the evidence base must stay independent of the design conclusions it supports, or it stops being evidence.

Why the honest gap matters

The empty §5 and the pending processing status are not weaknesses in the message -- they are part of what the message claims. SEJA's message addresses a designer who "is aware of what they have learned about their system's users, and equally aware of what they have not yet learned." A research file that records its own gaps is the message practising what it preaches: the epistemic posture is visible in the artifacts, not just asserted in prose. When a critique or a product-topology view later surfaces a research-to-design gap (the R-PS-002 scenario), it is comparing the evidence base against the design conclusions -- which only works because the two are kept as separate, honest layers.
