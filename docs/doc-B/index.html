SEJA Documentation Collection -- System-Isomorphic Edition

SEJA (Software Engineering with AI) is an agent harness that organizes human-AI collaboration through a system-isomorphic document architecture. This edition is written for practitioners who work with the harness day to day, and it is organized to mirror the system itself: each of the five numbered volumes maps to a distinct layer of SEJA -- onboarding, the design layer, the execution layer, output and governance, and the foundations. The intent is that the shape of the documentation matches the shape of what it describes, so once you know the system you already know where to look.

Each volume's chapters carry Diataxis section tags that classify content as tutorial, how-to, reference, or explanation. An audience-routing Reading Map helps you navigate to the content that matches your role and immediate need.

    Understanding SEJA's design philosophy: metacommunication, constitution, design intent, and markers.

    1. 1. The Metacommunication Message The one-utterance-of-many-voices message at the apex of the design layer -- how the designer's intent is encoded so the agent can receive it, and why every downstream artifact is a fragment of that single message.
    2. 2. The Constitution The immutable anchor of the design layer: how product-design/constitution.md records principles that do not change and constrains everything the agent is allowed to decide.
    3. 3. Design Intent Files The as-intended and as-coded views of design intent -- the living files that evolve as understanding deepens, how the two views are held in tension, and how drift between them is surfaced and reconciled.
    4. 4. The Marker System The mechanism that governs what changes and what stays fixed: how markers classify content by mutability and how tooling reads them to decide what the agent may safely touch.
    5. 5. UX Research Artifacts The evidence base beneath the design layer -- the research artifacts that ground design decisions in observed reality rather than assumption, and how they feed back into design intent.

  5. Vol. 5: Foundations Phase 2

    Directory: vol5/

    The intellectual foundations: semiotic engineering, sign systems, communicability, reflective practice, and the governable-and-epistemic thesis.

    1. 1. Software as Designed Communication The founding premise of semiotic engineering: software is a one-shot message from designers to users, and every interface is a communicative artifact carrying its designers' intent.
    2. 2. Sign Systems and Communicability Signs, sign systems, and communicability as the criterion by which a designed message succeeds or breaks down -- and the inspection methods that expose where meaning fails to cross.
    3. 3. The Extended Metacommunication Template Extends de Souza's metacommunication template to the agent setting, reframing the designer-to-user message as a designer-to-agent-to-user relay that the harness makes legible.
    4. 4. Reflective Practice Grounds SEJA's lifecycle in Schön's reflective practitioner: reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action as the discipline the harness operationalizes through its critique and reflection steps.
    5. 5. Abduction and Plural Readings Draws on Peirce's abduction and Eco's model reader to explain how the harness reasons toward the best available interpretation and why a single artifact sustains multiple legitimate readings.
    6. 6. The Governable AND Epistemic Thesis The central claim: SEJA is designed to be simultaneously governable (its actions constrained and auditable) and epistemic (its reasoning surfaced and inspectable), and the tension between the two.
    7. 7. How the Agent Reads the Harness Closes the loop: how the agent itself parses the harness as a sign system -- skills, rules, references, and markers -- turning the preceding theory into an account of the agent's own reading.

How to Read This

Diataxis Section Tags

Chapters in this collection use Diataxis-inspired section tags to classify content by its purpose. Each <section> element carries a data-diataxis attribute with one of four values:

tutorial
Learning-oriented: step-by-step lessons that guide you through a task for the first time.
how-to
Task-oriented: practical recipes for accomplishing specific goals when you already understand the basics.
reference
Information-oriented: precise, complete descriptions of components, APIs, or configuration options.
explanation
Understanding-oriented: conceptual discussions that clarify why things work the way they do.
