Volume 5: Foundations and Philosophy Phase 2

This volume is in draft status. Chapter content has been written for Phase 2; editorial review is pending.

This is the theoretical depth behind SEJA -- opt-in for every reader, essential for academics. It can be read linearly as a monograph or consulted by reference when a specific concept in the practitioner volumes needs its grounding made explicit. The seven chapters trace SEJA's intellectual lineage: semiotic engineering, the metacommunication template, reflective practice, abductive reading, and the twin claims that the harness is at once governable and epistemic.

The theory here is load-bearing, not decorative. The harness's design choices -- its markers, its lifecycle discipline, its insistence that software is a message from designers to users -- are consequences of these foundations, not ornaments bolted on afterward. Read this volume and the rest of the collection stops looking like a collection of conventions and starts looking like a single argument.

Each chapter also carries a critical counter-reading: an honest account of what the theory does not explain, where SEJA may over- or under-engineer, and which questions remain open. The foundations are offered as a working position, not a settled doctrine.

Chapters

  1. 1. Software as Designed Communication Establishes the founding premise of semiotic engineering: software is a one-shot message from designers to users, and every interface is a communicative artifact carrying its designers' intent.
  2. 2. Sign Systems and Communicability Introduces signs, sign systems, and communicability as the criterion by which a designed message succeeds or breaks down -- and the inspection methods that expose where meaning fails to cross.
  3. 3. The Extended Metacommunication Template Extends de Souza's metacommunication template to the agent setting, reframing the designer-to-user message as a designer-to-agent-to-user relay that the harness makes legible.
  4. 4. Reflective Practice Grounds SEJA's lifecycle in Schön's reflective practitioner: reflection-in-action and reflection-on-action as the discipline the harness operationalizes through critique and reflection steps.
  5. 5. Abduction and Plural Readings Draws on Peirce's abduction and Eco's model reader to explain how the harness reasons toward the best available interpretation and why a single artifact sustains multiple legitimate readings.
  6. 6. The Governable AND Epistemic Thesis Argues the central claim: SEJA is designed to be simultaneously governable (its actions constrained and auditable) and epistemic (its reasoning surfaced and inspectable), and examines the tension between the two.
  7. 7. How the Agent Reads the Harness Closes the loop: how the agent itself parses the harness as a sign system -- skills, rules, references, and markers -- turning the preceding theory into an account of the agent's own reading.

Recommended Reading Order

Read linearly for the monograph: Chapters 1 and 2 lay the semiotic groundwork, Chapter 3 carries it into the agent setting, and Chapters 4 and 5 add the practice and inference lenses. Chapter 6 states the thesis the whole volume builds toward, and Chapter 7 turns the theory back on the harness itself. Consulting by reference is equally valid -- each chapter stands alone and names the practitioner-volume concepts it grounds, so you can enter wherever a term sent you here.

Related Volumes

The concepts grounded here drive the practitioner volumes: the design artifacts of Vol. 2: The Design Layer and the execution machinery of Vol. 3: The Execution Layer. The governance side of the governable-and-epistemic thesis is realized in Vol. 4: The Output and Governance Layer. Newcomers should start with Vol. 1: Onboarding and Orientation before opting into this theoretical material.
