What it does

/reflect helps you reflect on specific work you have done, through two explicit lenses: the product lens (what you built — does it do what you intended?) and the practice lens (how you worked — what patterns emerge in how you designed and built it?). It records your own words verbatim; it is strictly non-prescriptive and never writes “you should” or “we recommend.”

Three modes are available. In conversational mode (the default), you pick a scope — recent plans, recent research, a specific artifact by ID, a time window, or free-form — the skill summarizes the chosen artifacts, asks which lens you are reflecting through, asks the lens-appropriate open-ended question, and saves your reflection. Deep mode (--deep) reads telemetry and briefs within a window and produces an event-matrix visualization, a transition graph, and a past-tense practice-evolution narrative. Telemetry mode (--telemetry) runs five statistical analysis primitives over usage data.

When to run it

Run /reflect when you want to step back and look at specific work you have done. Use the product lens to examine what you built — a feature, a design decision, a finished spec — and ask whether it does what you intended. Use the practice lens (Schön’s reflection-on-practice) to examine how you worked — the patterns that emerge, where you hesitated, what you would do differently.

Reach for --deep when you want a visual timeline and narrative chronicle of practice evolution across a period, and --telemetry when you want statistical pattern mining instead of narrative reflection.

How it fits the lifecycle

On the canonical path — /research → /design | /plan → /implement → /critique → /document | /communicate → /reflect — /reflect is the closing stage, the step back after work is delivered.

Reflection feeds the next cycle. Because it is deliberately non-prescriptive, its natural follow-ups are forward-looking: /research for a prescriptive take on any pattern surfaced here, /design to turn a surfaced pattern into new design intent, or /plan to turn it into new work.
