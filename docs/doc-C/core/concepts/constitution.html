What the constitution is

The constitution (product-design/constitution.md) is the set of a project's immutable principles — the non-negotiable commitments that every skill and agent must observe. For SEJA itself, those commitments span design authority, epistemic posture, privacy, lifecycle discipline, artifact integrity, and authorship voice. It is organised into Technical, Quality, Security, and Compliance principles, each stated with its rationale.

The constitution is not a wish list or a style guide; it is the artifact that other artifacts must not contradict. Its opening line states the rule plainly: rules here take precedence over everything else. No plan or implementation step may override a principle without an explicit, designer-approved decision record.

The immutability guarantee

“Immutable” here means immutable-by-default, not literally unwritable. The guarantee has two parts. First, precedence: when any other reference, plan, or instruction conflicts with the constitution, the constitution wins. Design intent is authoritative, and the constitution and the metacommunication message are the highest-authority artifacts in the project.

Second, controlled amendment: the constitution can be changed, but only through a deliberate governance act — the change must be explicitly approved by the project lead and recorded in the constitution's own changelog. It cannot be edited casually in the course of ordinary work. Because the file is human-authored, agents read it but do not rewrite it; they treat its principles as fixed constraints for the duration of every invocation.

How it is loaded

Precedence is only meaningful if the principles are actually present in the agent's context. The harness guarantees this structurally: pre-skill reads and injects the constitution on every invocation. It is the last stage of the pre-skill pipeline, and conceptually it is loaded first in authority — before conventions or any other reference file — so its rules frame everything that follows.

The constitution is also a pinned anchor: it must survive any context compaction. If a session's context is summarised or truncated, the constitution must be re-injected verbatim and must never be abbreviated. Dropping it would let an agent bypass the very governance rules the harness exists to enforce, so it is treated as non-compactable alongside the permission and constraint references and the active skill's instructions.

The trust boundaries it establishes

The constitution's principles draw the harness's trust boundaries — the lines an agent may not cross without explicit human authorisation. Four are worth naming:

  • Lifecycle discipline (T1). Every user-facing skill starts with pre-skill and ends with post-skill; a skill may opt out of non-critical stages but may never skip pre-skill entirely. Bypassing the lifecycle defeats the governance model.
  • Design-intent integrity (T2, Q1). Human (markers) files receive prose only from the designer; agents write only lifecycle markers, through apply_marker.py, after confirmation. Design intent is authoritative — no step may silently override it.
  • Artifact integrity (T3). Generated artifacts are agent-maintained and never hand-edited, so the machine-readable record cannot diverge from the harness's validation.
  • Privacy and hook integrity (S1, S2). Private content must never reach the public mirror without explicit opt-in, and no step may circumvent the pre-commit hook without a recorded, designer-approved decision.

These boundaries are enforced, not merely stated: the principles are loaded into every agent context via pre-skill, and violations found during /critique are classified as blocking — they must be resolved before commit.
