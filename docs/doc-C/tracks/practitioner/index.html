What SEJA is

SEJA is an agent harness for Claude Code, grounded in semiotic engineering. It is not a framework, a library, or a plugin. It is a governance layer that structures the lifecycle of AI-assisted software development around your intent as the designer.

When a project is governed by SEJA, it gains a stable design layer: a metacommunication message that expresses who your users are and what they need, a constitution of immutable principles, design-intent files that record the target state, and as-coded files that track what was actually built. The harness enforces the boundary between these layers so that plans serve design intent, implementations serve plans, and critiques evaluate against design intent — not against the AI's own heuristics. Your intent stays authoritative from the first research question through the final commit.

The three things SEJA gives you

SEJA rests on three value pillars.

Governance
A lifecycle of linked skills, enforced by pre-skill and post-skill hooks that wrap every skill you run. A constitution of immutable principles governs each invocation, and a configurable council of review perspectives evaluates your work. Throughout, your intent as the designer is authoritative.
Epistemic support
SEJA surfaces what is not yet known, not only what has been decided. Gap detection, heuristic labeling, explicit unknowns, and pending-actions tracking of unincorporated learning all work to make the boundary between the known and the unknown visible — rather than silently omitting it.
Lifecycle discipline
A structured progression from research to reflection, with artifacts linking each phase to the next. Plans serve design intent, implementations serve plans, and critiques evaluate against design intent. Nothing floats free of the phase before it.

What SEJA does NOT do

Knowing the boundaries is as important as knowing the value. SEJA:

  • does not replace your IDE;
  • does not auto-deploy;
  • does not make architectural decisions for you;
  • does not generate design intent without genuine designer engagement;
  • does not guarantee that your design intent is ethical — it guarantees only that the implementation matches the intent you expressed.
